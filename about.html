What is Wushu?

The art behind the sport

Placeholder text. Wushu (武术) is the modern, standardized form of Chinese martial arts. It combines athleticism, precision, and performance — blending deep stances, explosive jumps, and flowing weapon routines into choreographed sequences called forms, or taolu.

Placeholder text. Wushu is generally divided into two families. Changquan ("long fist") emphasizes speed, power, and aerial technique, while Nanquan ("southern fist") focuses on rooted stances and strong upper-body work. Internal styles such as Taijiquan emphasize balance, control, and breath.

Placeholder text. Alongside barehand forms, practitioners train traditional weapons — staff (gun), broadsword (dao), straightsword (jian), and spear (qiang) among them. Every routine is judged on difficulty, execution, and overall presentation.

Wushu at a glance

  • Taolu — choreographed forms, the focus of our club
  • Changquan — northern long fist styles
  • Nanquan — southern short-range styles
  • Taijiquan — slow, internal, balance-focused
  • Weapons — staff, broadsword, straightsword, spear
